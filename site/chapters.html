Chapters

The chapter is the basic unit of the League.

The League is organised into chapters: small communities of Statesmen who study, discuss and produce work together. A Statesman without a chapter is not yet in formation.

How chapters work

Chapters operate entirely online. Members do not need to be in the same city or on the same campus. The League is distributed by design, because the work of formation should not depend on geography.

Each chapter sets its own meeting day, chosen by its members. Meetings cover the current lecture topic, questions that came up during the week, and progress on the chapter's semester project. The Chapter Leader submits a report to the Founding Council after each meeting.

Chapter Leaders

Every chapter is led by a Chapter Leader. The Chapter Leader facilitates weekly meetings, keeps the chapter engaged with the formation track, and is the point of accountability between the chapter and the Founding Council.

Chapter Leaders are not only administrators. They are themselves in formation, expected to engage as seriously with the curriculum as any member of their chapter, and to model what that engagement looks like.

Joining a chapter

When you register as a Statesman, you will be placed in a chapter that fits your schedule and your functional group. Chapter placement is not optional. It is part of what you are signing up for.

The lectures matter. But the chapter meeting is where they take root.
