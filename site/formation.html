Formation

Formation is the work of becoming.

It is not learning facts or earning credentials. It is being shaped in conviction, in habit and in the capacity for sustained, principled action in public life.

The curriculum

The Statesmen League runs a five-phase formation track. Each phase builds on the one before it.

The curriculum begins with foundational questions: who is a statesman, what does character mean, what does it cost, and what does it produce. It then moves into the practical side of governance: public speech, policy thinking, civic responsibility and the specific demands of Nigerian public life.

We are currently in the pre-formation season. Every chapter is studying the Ideal Statesman and Stateswoman and the scriptural archetypes, Daniel, David, Deborah and Esther, before the formal curriculum opens. The founding team completes each topic first, then releases it to chapters.

How it works

Lectures are delivered on Sundays via Zoom by the founding team. Chapter members follow the recording of each lecture and engage with the material in their own time. Each chapter then meets, on a day it sets itself, to discuss the lecture, work through questions and progress its semester project.

Formation happens in community. Watching a lecture alone is not enough. The chapter meeting is where the material becomes conviction.

Functional groups

Every Statesman and Stateswoman belongs to one of three functional groups, based on their gifts and their point of engagement with public life.

Writers and Publishers

Producing long-form articles and short written content in the League's name, adding to the public conversation on governance and character.

Content Makers

Producing video content that carries the League's ideas to a wider audience and makes the work of formation visible.

Campus Politics

Active in student union and campus governance, practising statecraft at the level immediately available to them.

Formation is not spectator work. Every group has a role in the League's output.

Chapter output

Each chapter is expected to produce at least one long article per topic covered, publish shorter written or video content during the week, and complete one chapter project per semester. The project is practical: something done in the chapter's own context that shows what formation looks like in action.

Formation begins in a chapter.
